## Step 1: Translate Actions → Outcomes

**Objective:** Transform action-oriented statements into measurable business outcomes.

### Process

1. Identify all action statements in the user's content
2. For each action, ask "Why?" repeatedly until reaching the business outcome
3. Document the outcome without implementation details

### Transformation Examples

| Action (Operational) | Outcome (Executive) |
|---|---|
| "We will implement a new CRM system" | "We will increase customer retention by 15%" |
| "We will reorganize the sales team" | "We will reduce close time from 45 to 30 days" |
| "We will create new training processes" | "We will improve team productivity by 25%" |
| "We will migrate to cloud infrastructure" | "We will reduce infrastructure costs by 40% while improving uptime to 99.9%" |
| "We will hire 5 new engineers" | "We will accelerate product delivery from quarterly to monthly releases" |

### Signals of Operational Thinking to Flag

- Statements starting with "We will implement/create/build/deploy..."
- Lists of tasks or action items without stated purpose
- Technical specifications without business context
- Timelines without milestones tied to outcomes
- Resource requests without ROI framing

### What to Communicate to the User

- Which actions were identified
- Why they are problematic in an executive context
- What the real business outcome is

## Step 2: Identify Frictions & False Beliefs

**Objective:** Anticipate objections that would kill the decision before they are voiced.

### Definitions

- **Friction:** Any factor the executive believes will make implementation or success difficult. Frictions are often real constraints (budget, capacity, timeline).
- **False Belief:** Any assumption the executive holds about why the idea will not work, even though it is not objectively true. False beliefs stem from past experiences, industry myths, or incomplete information.

### Common Executive Objections to Scan For

| Objection | Type | Why Executives Think This |
|---|---|---|
| "We tried this before and it didn't work" | False Belief | Past failure assumed to predict future failure, ignoring changed conditions |
| "Our customers don't want this" | False Belief | Anecdotal feedback generalized to entire customer base |
| "This will require too many resources" | Friction | Real constraint, but often overestimated without data |
| "The team doesn't have capacity" | Friction | Real constraint requiring phasing or prioritization |
| "The market isn't ready" | False Belief | Timing assumption without supporting evidence |
| "This is too risky" | Friction | Perceived risk often exceeds actual risk |
| "We don't have the right talent" | Friction | Capability gap that may be addressable |

### Prioritization Criteria

Rank objections by:

1. **Likelihood:** How probable is it that this objection will surface?
2. **Impact:** If raised, would it kill the decision or just slow it?
3. **Addressability:** Can you neutralize it with evidence or framing?

Address the top 2-3 objections. Focus on high-likelihood, high-impact objections that are addressable.

### What to Communicate to the User

- Which objections are anticipated and why
- How each is categorized (friction vs. false belief)
- Which are most critical to address and in what order
